Preorders Top $1 Billion, Launch Date Holds — What's Actually Confirmed About Take-Two Right Now

Published September 30, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026 · 8 min read

Every few weeks, a new claim shows up online about GTA VI's business side — a delay, a new online mode date, "official" virtual land you can buy. We track this so you don't have to sort real news from rumors. Here's where things actually stand as of today.

This article covers publicly available business and stock information. It's not financial advice, and we're not financial advisors — always do your own research before making any investment decision. It's separate from our buying guides, which do contain affiliate links (see our disclosure page).

Update — November 14

GTA VI launches in five days. The November 19 date is holding, preloading is live, and Take-Two has reported its latest earnings. Here is what changed.

Preorders are far bigger than Take-Two planned for

On the November 9 earnings call, Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick said GTA VI preorders are "tracking far in excess of our initial expectations." Take-Two has not given an official preorder number.

The numbers you see online come from Sensor Tower, an outside research firm. They are estimates, not company figures:

  • November 3: about 7.7 million preorders, worth about $751 million
  • November 12: about 11.3 million preorders, worth about $1.09 billion
  • November 13: more than 12 million preorders, worth about $1.18 billion

For scale, GTA V had about 7 million preorders. About three out of four GTA VI preorders are on PS5. The average works out to roughly $97 a copy, which points to a lot of buyers choosing the $99.99 Ultimate Edition. Sony and Microsoft have each said GTA VI is the most preordered game ever on their stores.

Take-Two's earnings: a weak quarter, a steady outlook

Take-Two reported on November 9. The quarter itself was soft. The full-year plan did not change.

  • Bookings for the quarter were $1.6 billion. That is down 18% from a year ago and just under the company's own target of $1.62–$1.67 billion. Take-Two blamed NBA 2K, which sold well but below plan.
  • Spending inside its existing games fell 9%. The company had expected a 5% drop.
  • The full-year bookings forecast stayed at $8.0–$8.2 billion. The revenue forecast moved up slightly, to $7.98–$8.18 billion.
  • The launch quarter is expected to bring in $3.35–$3.5 billion in bookings. That is about double the same quarter last year.

Take-Two's stock rose about 8% the day after the report. Zelnick also said he does not want to "claim success before it occurs." That is fair. Preorders are not the same as launch sales, and some of them may just be sales that would have happened on day one anyway.

PS5 passes 100 million

Sony says it has shipped more than 100 million PS5 consoles as of November 10. "Shipped" means sent to stores, not bought by players. Some analysts also warn that one game, even this one, may only do so much for console sales in a weak year for hardware.

Still not real: GTA VI land, tokens, and marketplaces

Nothing has changed here. Rockstar has not announced creator payouts, a marketplace, virtual property you can resell, or any NFT or token. There is still no PC date and no GTA VI Online date. With launch this close, expect more fake offers, not fewer.

Update — October 12

Nothing big has changed since September 30. The November 19 launch date still stands, and there is still no official GTA VI Online date, PC date, creator payout program, marketplace, or transferable virtual property. Rockstar's most recent listed announcements are still its October 8 music and merchandise releases.

The one new item is from Wall Street. On October 12, Wells Fargo kept its "Overweight" rating (a buy-leaning rating) and its $300 price target on Take-Two. That is a repeat of its view, not an upgrade. Two details from the note are worth knowing:

  • Wells Fargo still expects GTA VI to sell about 50 million copies at an average price near $90. That is higher than the roughly 37 million copies built into the average analyst estimate.
  • It also expects softer near-term numbers. The firm trimmed its estimates for Take-Two's current quarter, blaming lower NBA 2K sales, and expects the company's next guidance to come in below what analysts expect.

The wider picture, from an aggregate of 29 analysts: the average rating is "Strong Buy," the average price target is about $286, and the average forecast for the fiscal year that ends March 2027 is about $8.5 billion in revenue and $6.87 in adjusted earnings per share. These are estimates, not promises.

One more thing to watch: a leak threat tied to cryptocurrency has been reported. We found no verified proof that a full playable build of GTA VI has been released. If you see a "leaked GTA VI download," don't open it. Fake downloads are a common way to spread malware.

The one real update from September 30

A new GTA VI magazine feature went live with developer interviews and 12 new screenshots. That's the marketing beat we were expecting. Nothing else about the launch date or online mode changed alongside it.

What Wall Street is saying about Take-Two

Take-Two Interactive (ticker: TTWO) is the company behind Rockstar Games and GTA VI. On September 28, JPMorgan kept its "Overweight" (buy-leaning) rating on the stock with a $310 price target. That's a reaffirmation of an existing view, not a new upgrade — it didn't change the overall picture much.

Take-Two's own guidance for its next fiscal year (FY2027, which covers most of the GTA VI launch window) still points to roughly $8.0–$8.2 billion in bookings — a term the company uses similar to "sales." Outside analysts' average estimates land close to that too, around $8.5 billion in revenue and about 37 million GTA VI copies sold baked into those numbers.

One way to read how "expensive" a stock is compares its price to its expected earnings — a lower number generally means investors are paying less per dollar of profit. Take-Two is currently trading at roughly 29–32 times its expected earnings, versus about 16 times for the gaming industry as a whole. In plain terms: the stock price already assumes GTA VI is going to be a very big hit. That cuts both ways — it leaves less room for a pleasant surprise, and more room to disappoint if sales come in lower than expected.

The soft spot in the business

Away from GTA VI, some of Take-Two's existing games have cooled off. In the quarter that ended in September, spending by players inside its existing games fell 9%. The company had expected a 5% drop. NBA 2K and mobile games were both down from a year ago. GTA VI is carrying a lot of the growth story right now.

What to watch next

The next big date is launch day, November 19. After that, watch for Take-Two's first official sales numbers. Zelnick has said the company will update investors after launch. Those numbers will show whether the preorder estimates were right.

  • First-day and first-week sales, straight from Take-Two
  • Reviews, and any server or performance problems at launch
  • Whether PS5 and Xbox consoles sell out again
  • Any official word on GTA VI Online or a PC version
